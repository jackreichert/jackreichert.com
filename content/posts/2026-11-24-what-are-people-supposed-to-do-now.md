With AI moving into, and dominating, more and more areas the question arises: what do we need people for?

If models are solving math problems that have been unsolved for 160 years, why do we need mathematicians?

If art can be generated at the speed of thought, why do we need artists?

If reams of documents can be written on any topic within the blink of an eye, why bother writing yourself?

The answer to this is deeply rooted in the age-old question of what is the meaning of life.

I wouldn’t quit an art class if I saw that the teacher could draw better than I know I ever would. I also wouldn’t stop weight-lifting knowing that the strongest man can never come close to lifting what an excavator can easily lift. Nor would I stop writing essays just because I can prompt one instantaneously.

Living is hard. Being human is hard. Learning and growing is hard. That is part of the point. The difficulty isn’t the point, but the striving very much is.

Stress is what builds muscles, and physical strength is what helps us live healthy lives.  
Struggling through a difficult book is what expands our abilities to think.

AI is becoming the excavator of so many things. Yet we still have excavator operators.

There is a fear that people will be superseded by AI, that we will no longer be needed. That fear is legitimate. The “computers” during WWII were people computing and code breaking, and there was a time we needed the entire community hunting and gathering just for that community to survive. Society changed with the tools we built. Sometimes for the better, sometimes not.

While I’m unsure what the future job market will look like, and I do believe there will be a healthy one for a long time to come, I am certain of some things.

Just as exercise is necessary for people to do regularly to grow strong and stay healthy, reading challenging books will always make people better thinkers, just as reading fiction will expand your perspectives of the world, and your soul.

Creating art, as the expression of the human soul, is a uniquely human endeavor — no matter how easily generative AI can “draw” the same exact thing. I would mourn deeply if artists stopped creating.

I believe that the purpose of any person is to try to live life the best way they can, and while doing so, help make the world around them better. AI can be an excavator to this end, but it cannot replace the striving needed to grow, to become that person that you can be.
